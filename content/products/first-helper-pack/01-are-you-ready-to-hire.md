# Are You Ready to Hire?

Hiring too early is one of the most common ways a profitable solo business turns unprofitable. Use this checklist and the calculator before you post a job.

---

## Readiness checklist

**Demand (need at least 3 of 4)**
- [ ] You've turned away or waitlisted work for **6+ of the last 8 weeks**
- [ ] Your calendar is booked **2+ weeks out** consistently
- [ ] You have recurring clients (predictable hours to give a helper)
- [ ] You have jobs that are clearly faster or safer with two people

**Pricing & margin (need all 3)**
- [ ] Your prices hit your target hourly rate (check with the Your Numbers / Hourly Target sheets)
- [ ] You know your profit per job, not just revenue
- [ ] The **Does It Pay** sheet shows positive net per week after ramp-up

**Systems (need at least 3 of 4)**
- [ ] Written checklists for your main services
- [ ] A scheduling system someone else can read
- [ ] Clear quality standards (what "done" looks like, with photos)
- [ ] A way to pay someone legally (payroll service quote in hand)

**You (be honest)**
- [ ] You're willing to spend 4–8 weeks training instead of just doing the work
- [ ] You can afford 2–3 months of helper cost before the extra revenue fully arrives
- [ ] You're okay with work being done 90% your way

**Score:** if you can't check the Pricing & margin section completely, **raise prices before hiring.** A helper multiplies whatever your margin already is — including a bad one.

## The math (use `08-helper-cost-calculator.xlsx`)

1. **Loaded cost per hour** — wage + employer payroll taxes + workers' comp + payroll service + gear + vehicle costs. Default example: $18/hr wage → about **$22.66/hr loaded**.
2. **Revenue per labor hour** you bill (job price ÷ labor hours). Default: $60.
3. **Helper productivity vs. you:** new helpers typically produce 50–70% of what you do in the first months; 75–90% once trained.
4. **Break-even productivity** = loaded cost ÷ revenue per labor hour. Default: **38%** — the helper must produce at least 38% of what you do per hour just to cover their cost.
5. **Can you fill their hours?** If not, the helper costs you money no matter how good they are.

## Two ways a helper pays off

| Model | How it works | Best for |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| **Team jobs** | You work together; jobs finish faster; you fit more jobs per day | Cleaning, lawn, pressure washing, detailing large vehicles |
| **Split routes** | Helper handles simpler jobs alone while you do complex or high-value work | Once they're fully trained and trusted |

The **Team Job Pricing** sheet shows what team jobs do to your earnings per hour. Rule: **keep the job price the same** — the client pays for the result, not the headcount.

## Alternatives to hiring (try these first)
- Raise prices until demand matches your capacity
- Drop your least profitable clients or services
- Tighten routes (less drive time = more billable hours)
- Outsource admin (bookkeeping, scheduling) before field work
