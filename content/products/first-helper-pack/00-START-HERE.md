# Benchline Hiring Your First Helper Pack — Start Here

> **Not legal, tax, or HR advice.** Employment law (worker classification, minimum wage, overtime, payroll taxes, workers' compensation, new-hire reporting, background checks) varies by state and changes over time. Talk to an accountant and, if needed, an employment attorney before you hire.

Your first helper can double what you can take on — or eat your margin and your weekends. This pack walks through the decision, the hire, and the first 30 days.

## Work through it in this order

| Step | File | Time |
|------|------|------|
| 1. Decide if you're ready | `01-are-you-ready-to-hire.md` + `08-helper-cost-calculator.xlsx` | 45 min |
| 2. Understand employee vs. contractor | `02-employee-vs-contractor-overview.md` → book a call with your accountant | 20 min + call |
| 3. Post the job | `03-job-post-templates.md` | 30 min |
| 4. Interview and trial | `04-interview-and-trial-day.md` | per candidate |
| 5. Onboard | `05-onboarding-checklist.md` | first week |
| 6. Train | `06-training-sop-template.md` | write 3 SOPs before day one |
| 7. Check quality | `07-quality-check-form.md` | weekly for the first month |

## Before the helper's first day (minimum)
- [ ] Classification decided with your accountant (W-2 employee is the default for most helpers)
- [ ] Payroll set up (payroll service, state registration, new-hire reporting)
- [ ] Workers' compensation coverage in place if required in your state (and often wise even if not)
- [ ] Your general liability / commercial auto insurer knows you're adding a worker
- [ ] Three written SOPs for the tasks they'll do most
- [ ] Safety gear in their size
