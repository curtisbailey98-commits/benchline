# Price Increase Notices

**Rules:** 30 days' notice minimum (check your service agreements for required notice). Say what's changing, when, and thank them. One honest reason is enough — don't over-explain. Send individually, not as a group text.

---

## Text — recurring client (standard)

> Hi [Name], a quick heads-up: starting [date], your [weekly/biweekly] [service] will be $[new] (currently $[old]). My costs for [fuel / insurance / supplies] have gone up and I want to keep giving you the same quality and reliability. Thank you for being a client — I really appreciate you.

## Text — long-time client (softer)

> Hi [Name], you've been with me since [year] and I'm grateful. I'm adjusting my prices for the first time in [time], effective [date]. Your new price will be $[new] per visit (from $[old]). Happy to answer any questions.

## Text — client significantly underpriced (bigger jump)

> Hi [Name], I want to be upfront: your price has fallen well below my current rate for similar [homes/yards/vehicles]. Starting [date], your visit price will be $[new]. I know that's a real change, so if you'd like to adjust the scope instead — [option, e.g., every other week, fewer add-ons] — I'm glad to work that out with you.

## Email — all clients (annual update)

**Subject:** Your [Business] pricing for [year]

Hi [Name],

Thank you for trusting [Business] with your [home/yard/car] this past year.

Starting **[date]**, my prices are changing:

- Your [service]: **$[new]** per visit (currently $[old])
- [Any other service change]

This helps cover higher costs for [fuel, insurance, and equipment] while keeping the [on-time arrival / consistent quality / insured, written guarantees] you're used to.

Nothing else about your service is changing. If you have any questions, just reply to this email or text me at [phone].

Thank you again,
[Your name]
[Business]

## In person (at the end of a visit)

> "Before I go — I wanted to tell you in person that my prices are going up starting [date]. Yours will be $[new]. I'll send it in writing too. I really appreciate having you as a client."

## Handling replies

**"That's too much, I'll have to cancel."**
> I understand, and I appreciate you telling me. If a smaller scope or [less frequent schedule] would work, I'm happy to adjust. If not, thank you for being a great client — the door's always open.

**"Can you keep my old price?"**
> I appreciate you asking. I've kept prices the same for [time], so I'm applying the new rate to everyone. I hope you'll stay — you're a client I really like working with.

**Silence** → no response needed; the notice stands. Mention it once more at the last visit before the change.

## Timing tips
- **Best months:** January (cleaning, detailing, handyman) or February, before the season (lawn, pressure washing).
- **Avoid** announcing right after a complaint or a missed visit.
- **New clients** get the new price immediately; the notice period is for existing clients.
