# Pricing Conversations

How to present a price, handle pushback, and stop discounting — without sounding like a salesperson.

---

## 1. Presenting a price

**Rules**
1. **Scope first, price second.** Restate what they get before the number.
2. **Say the number once, clearly, then stop talking.**
3. **Offer options, not discounts.** Good / better / best, or "here's what we can do at a lower price."
4. **Put it in writing the same day.**

**Script**
> "So for your [house/yard/car], you'd get [scope in plain words — 3 items max]. That's **$[price]**. I can do it [day]. Want me to put you on the schedule?"

Then wait.

## 2. "That's more than I expected."

Don't defend or drop the price. Ask a question.

> "Totally fair. What were you expecting?" *(Listen.)* "Got it. The difference is usually [what's included / time / insurance / showing up on time]. If budget is the main thing, I could [remove X / do Y only] for $[lower number]. Would that work better?"

**You changed the scope, not your rate.** That's the difference between an option and a discount.

## 3. "The other guy quoted $X."

> "That might be a good fit for you. I can't speak to their quote, but mine includes [specific things: insurance, cleanup, written agreement, satisfaction policy, specific steps]. If you'd like, compare them line by line — and if they're doing the same work, go with the better price."

Confidence reads as quality. Clients who pick purely on price are also the most likely to complain and cancel.

## 4. "Can you do it cheaper if I pay cash?"

> "My price is the same however you pay — it's already what I need to do the job right."

(Cash discounts to avoid taxes are a legal risk to you, not just a pricing problem.)

## 5. "Can you give me a deal if I sign up for weekly?"

This is the one discount that makes sense, because recurring work lowers your costs. Use a pre-set recurring price, not a negotiated one.

> "Yes — recurring clients get my recurring rate: $[__] per visit instead of $[__]. That's the best price I offer."

## 6. "I'll think about it."

> "Of course. I'll send the written estimate now. I'm holding [day] for you until [date] — after that I'll open it up to other clients. Any questions I can answer now?"

Then follow up in 2–3 days (see Client Retention Text Pack or your Core Kit follow-up scripts).

## 7. Stop discounting — replacements that work

| Instead of… | Offer… |
|-------------|--------|
| 10% off | A smaller scope at a lower total |
| "I'll throw it in for free" | Include it as a named bonus on a larger package |
| Matching a lower quote | A written list of what yours includes |
| First-time discount | A clear first-visit price and a lower recurring price |
| Holiday sale on your core service | Gift certificates at full price; seasonal packages |

## 8. When to walk away

- They want a firm price without letting you see or photograph the job.
- They push for a discount before you've finished describing the scope.
- The Job Profit Check says "raise the price or pass" and they won't adjust scope.

> "I don't think I'm the right fit for this one, but I appreciate you reaching out."
