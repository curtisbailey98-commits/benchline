# How to Use the Calculators

A walkthrough of every input, using the default numbers in the spreadsheet as a worked example.

---

## 1 · Hourly Target

**Monthly business costs.** List everything the business pays for, even if it's irregular — divide annual bills by 12. Two lines people forget:
- **Equipment replacement fund:** mowers, pressure washers, vacuums, and vans wear out. Estimate replacement cost ÷ useful life in months.
- **Health insurance and retirement:** if you don't pay them, nobody will.

Default total: **$2,480/month ($29,760/year).**

**Take-home goal ($55,000), tax set-aside (25%).** The sheet grosses up your goal for taxes: $55,000 ÷ 0.75 = $73,333 of profit needed.

**Working weeks (47), billable hours (28/week).** Billable = on-site, paid time. Driving, quoting, invoicing, and buying supplies are not billable. Be honest — most solo operators bill 25–32 hours in a 45-hour week.

**Results with the defaults:**
- Revenue needed: $73,333 + $29,760 = **$103,093/year**
- Minimum rate: $103,093 ÷ (47 × 28 = 1,316 hrs) = **$78.34/hour**
- Target with 15% buffer: **$90.09/hour**
- What you actually earn per hour worked (including 14 non-billable hours/week): **$27.86** — which is why the billable rate has to be much higher than an employee wage.

**What to do with it:** every quote should work out to at least your target rate per billable hour. If the market truly won't pay it, change the inputs you control: raise billable hours (tighter routes, fewer gaps), cut costs, or grow average ticket with add-ons.

## 2 · Job Profit Check

Run it on any quote where you're unsure. Inputs: quoted price, your on-site hours, round-trip drive time, materials, disposal/rental fees, miles, cost per mile, card fees, helper hours and cost.

**Default example:** $350 job, 3.5 hours on site + 0.75 hours driving, $25 materials, 18 miles.
- Direct costs: $48.05 → gross profit $301.95 (86% margin)
- **Profit per hour of your time (including drive): $71.05** vs. target $90.09
- Verdict: **Raise the price or pass**
- Price needed to hit target: **$435**

> Gross margin looks great (86%) — but margin isn't the problem for solo operators. **Profit per hour of your time** is. That's why the verdict uses it.

## 3 · Drive Time Cost

For each zone, enter round-trip miles and minutes. The sheet costs the trip at your cost per mile + your target hourly rate for the drive time. It then subtracts a **free-travel allowance** (default 30 round-trip minutes) and suggests a trip fee for the rest.

**Default:** Zone 1 (25 min) → no fee · Zone 2 (40 min) → $20 · Zone 3 (60 min) → $60 · Zone 4 (85 min) → $105.

Use it to decide:
- Your **free service radius** (usually where the trip fee would be $0)
- **Trip fees** for outer zones — or a minimum job price that's higher in outer zones
- When to say no

**Cost per mile:** use your real vehicle cost per mile (fuel + maintenance + insurance + depreciation ÷ annual miles), or use the current IRS standard mileage rate as a reasonable proxy.

## 4 · Break-Even

Pulls monthly costs and your pay goal from sheet 1. Enter your average job price and variable cost per job.

**Default:** $225 average job, $30 variable cost → $195 contribution per job.
- **13 jobs/month** covers business costs
- **45 jobs/month (~10.4/week)** covers costs + your pay

If 10 jobs a week isn't realistic in your trade, you need a higher average ticket (bundles, add-ons, packages) or recurring clients.

## 5 · Price Increase Planner

**Default:** 40 recurring clients at $150/visit, 20 visits/year each = $120,000/year.
- 8% increase → new price $162
- If you lose 5% of clients: **$123,120/year (+$3,120)** and **100 hours freed**
- **Revenue-neutral client loss: 7.4%** — you'd have to lose more than 3 of 40 clients before an 8% increase costs you money, and even then you'd have more free hours

Most operators overestimate how many clients will leave over a modest, well-communicated increase. Use the notices in `04-price-increase-notices.md`.

## A 20-minute annual pricing review (every January)

1. Update costs on sheet 1 (insurance renewals, fuel, software).
2. Check last year's actual billable hours (from your calendar/invoices) — update the input.
3. Compare your target rate to your current prices using sheet 2 on 3 typical jobs.
4. If you're below target: plan an increase with sheet 5 and send notices 30 days ahead.
