# Handyman Client Intake Questions

The goal: know enough to quote from your desk (or know that you need to see it), and filter out jobs you shouldn't take.

---

## Contact
1. Name, mobile, email, job address
2. Owner or renter? (Renters need landlord approval for most work)
3. How did you find us?

## The work
4. What do you need done? List everything — even small items. (Bundling saves you money.)
5. For each item, can you text a photo? For mounting, include the wall; for fixtures, include the existing one and the new one's box/model number.
6. Who is supplying materials/fixtures — you or us? Do you already have them?
7. Any deadline? (Guests, listing a home, move-in date)

## The house
8. Approximate age of the home? (Pre-1978 = possible lead paint; pre-1980s = possible asbestos in some materials)
9. Wall type where we'll work: drywall, plaster, brick, tile?
10. For plumbing: do you know where the main water shutoff is? Any existing leaks?
11. For electrical tasks (where permitted): is there an existing box/fixture, and are breakers labeled?
12. Ceiling height where we'll work?
13. Parking and access: stairs, elevator, gate code, HOA rules on work hours?
14. Pets or kids at home during the work?

## Filters (ask directly)
15. Has anyone else started this work? (Fixing someone else's half-done job takes longer)
16. Is this an insurance claim or dispute with a contractor? (You may not want to be in the middle)
17. Budget range you had in mind? (Helps you propose options, not to lowball)

## Close

> "Thanks. I'll send a written estimate by [time]. My minimum visit is $[___], which covers the first hour, so it's usually best to bundle a few items into one visit. Anything else you want me to add?"

**Visit in person before quoting when:** water damage or leaks, anything "sagging," doors that "stick" (could be foundation movement), plaster repairs, or anything the client can't photograph well.

**Decline or refer when:** the work exceeds your license limits, needs a permit you can't pull, or the client wants something hidden rather than fixed.
