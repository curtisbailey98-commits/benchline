# Cleaning Pricing Guide

How to price residential cleaning so every visit covers your time, supplies, drive, and the job you didn't get because you were there.

---

## 1. Start from your hourly target, not from competitors

Open `02-cleaning-pricing-calculator.xlsx` → **Your Numbers**. With the default inputs ($50k take-home, $9k costs, 30 billable hours × 48 weeks) the target is about **$60/hour**. That is the number every price below should hit.

> If your target hourly rate is way above what cleaners charge locally, the fix is usually **billable hours** (tighter routes, fewer gaps) or **costs** — not working for less.

## 2. Pick a pricing method per service

| Service | Best method | Why |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| Recurring (weekly / biweekly / monthly) | **Flat rate per visit**, set from timed rooms | Clients want a predictable number; you get faster over time and keep the gain |
| First visit / deep clean | **Flat rate from room count × deep minutes** | Deep cleans vary; room counts keep it objective |
| Move-out / move-in | **Higher of sq ft price or time price** | Empty homes hide buildup (ovens, cabinets, baseboards) |
| Clutter-heavy, hoarding-adjacent, post-construction | **Hourly with a minimum and a not-to-exceed estimate** | Scope is impossible to see from photos |

Avoid pure square-foot pricing for recurring cleans — a 2,000 sq ft home with one bathroom cleans faster than a 1,400 sq ft home with three.

## 3. Time benchmarks (one experienced solo cleaner)

These are the defaults in the Quote Builder. **Time yourself for two weeks** and replace them.

| Area | Standard (min) | Deep (min) |
|------|----------------|-----------|
| Full bathroom | 25 | 45 |
| Half bathroom | 12 | 20 |
| Kitchen | 35 | 75 |
| Bedroom | 15 | 30 |
| Living / family room | 20 | 35 |
| Dining room | 10 | 20 |
| Office / den | 12 | 25 |
| Hallway / stairs (per floor) | 8 | 15 |
| Laundry room | 8 | 15 |
| Entry / mudroom | 5 | 10 |

Multipliers used in the sheet:
- **Condition:** 1.0 tidy · 1.2 average buildup · 1.4 heavy buildup or clutter
- **Shedding pets:** +10%
- **Move-out:** deep time × 1.15 (inside cabinets, closets, baseboards all fully exposed)

## 4. First visit = deep clean

Quote the first visit as a deep clean, then recurring visits at the standard price. Say it plainly:

> "The first clean is a reset — it takes about twice as long because we're catching up on buildup. After that, every-two-weeks visits are $[X]."

If they refuse the deep clean, quote the recurring price **after** two visits and tell them the first two will run long.

## 5. Recurring discounts

| Frequency | Discount off standard price | Reason |
|-----------|----------------------------|--------|
| Weekly | 10–15% | Least buildup, most predictable revenue |
| Every 2 weeks | 5–10% | The most common schedule |
| Every 4 weeks | 0–5% | Almost a deep clean every time |
| One-time | 0% (or quote as deep) | No repeat revenue |

The sheet applies 15% / 10% / 5% by default — lower these if your time benchmarks are tight.

## 6. Minimums and add-ons

- **Minimum visit:** never below 2 hours × your target rate (default $120). Small apartments still cost you a full drive and setup.
- **Add-ons** (inside oven, fridge, cabinets, interior windows, blinds, laundry, linens) are where margin lives. List them on every estimate, even if the client says no this time. Prices are in `03-cleaning-rate-card.csv`.
- **Supplies:** include your standard supplies in the price. If the client wants specific products, they supply them.

## 7. When to raise prices

Raise recurring prices when any of these are true:
- Your calendar is full for 3+ weeks and you're turning away inquiries
- A client's home consistently runs 20%+ over the time you quoted
- It's been 12 months since the last increase (5–8% is normal)

Give 30 days' notice in writing. Scripts are in `08-cleaning-text-scripts.md`.

## 8. Quick sanity check before you send any quote

`Quoted price ÷ estimated hours ≥ your target hourly rate`

The Quote Builder shows **Effective hourly** under the quote. If it's below target, raise the price, remove scope, or say no.
