# Move-Out / Move-In Cleaning Pricing

Move-outs are the most profitable one-time job in residential cleaning — and the easiest to underprice. Empty homes expose everything: inside every cabinet, every baseboard, the oven nobody has cleaned in three years.

Use the **Move-Out Pricing** sheet in `02-cleaning-pricing-calculator.xlsx`.

---

## 1. The method: higher of two prices

1. **Square-foot price** = home sq ft × rate for condition
2. **Time price** = (sq ft ÷ your sq ft per hour) × target hourly rate
3. **Base price** = the higher of the two
4. **Quote** = base + add-ons, never below your move-out minimum

Default condition tiers (edit to your pace and market):

| Condition | What it looks like | Solo sq ft / hour | Starting $ / sq ft |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|--------------------|
| **Light** | Tenant cleaned, well-kept, < 2 years occupancy | 450 | $0.20 |
| **Average** | Normal living, some grease and soap scum | 350 | $0.27 |
| **Heavy** | Long occupancy, pets, smoking, heavy grease, sticky floors | 250 | $0.38 |

If the home still has furniture, the sheet adds 25% time (you're cleaning around things).

**Minimum move-out price:** default $250. A 600 sq ft studio still needs inside-appliance and cabinet work.

## 2. What a move-out includes (put this on the estimate)

Everything in the deep clean **plus**:
- Inside all kitchen cabinets and drawers (wiped, crumbs vacuumed)
- Inside bathroom vanities and medicine cabinets
- Inside closets: shelves, rods, floors
- All baseboards, door frames, doors (both sides), and switch plates
- Window sills and tracks (interior)
- Light fixtures (reachable, bugs removed)
- Floors fully cleaned, including under where appliances can be pulled out (only if safe to move)

**Usually add-ons:** inside oven, inside refrigerator, interior window glass, blinds, wall washing, garage, balcony/patio.

> Many landlords and property managers have their own checklist. Ask for it before quoting — a missing item on their list can cost your client part of their deposit, and that gets blamed on you.

## 3. Quoting from photos

Ask for photos of: inside the oven, inside the fridge, the worst bathroom's shower, and the kitchen floor. If they won't send photos:
- Quote **Average** condition and state in writing: "Price assumes average condition. If the home needs heavy-condition work, we'll confirm a revised price before starting."

## 4. Conditions to put in writing

- **Utilities on:** water, electricity, and hot water must be working. No utilities = reschedule fee.
- **Home empty:** the price assumes the home is empty. Leftover items or trash removal is quoted separately (or declined).
- **Deposits and inspections:** "We clean to a professional standard, but we can't guarantee the return of a security deposit — inspections involve damage, wear, and landlord judgment outside our control."
- **Re-clean window:** missed items reported within **24 hours of the walkthrough**, before new occupants move in.
- **Payment:** move-outs are one-time — take a deposit (25–50%) to book and the balance on completion, before keys change hands.

## 5. Worked example (default sheet inputs)

- 1,400 sq ft, Average condition, empty home, target rate ≈ $60/hr
- Square-foot price: 1,400 × $0.27 = **$378**
- Time price: 1,400 ÷ 350 = 4.0 hr × $60 = **$242**
- Base = $378
- Add-ons: oven $45, fridge $40, cabinets $75, 10 windows × $8 = $80 → $240
- **Quote: $620** · Estimated time 7.6 hours · Effective hourly ≈ $82

## 6. Sell move-outs to the people who book them repeatedly

- **Property managers** and small landlords (turnovers every year)
- **Real estate agents** (pre-listing cleans, move-in cleans as closing gifts)
- **Apartment communities** that don't have in-house cleaners

Pitch: a flat per-bedroom move-out price list, 48-hour turnaround, and photos after every clean. One property manager can be worth 20+ jobs a year.
