# Job Pricing System
## Know what to charge before you accept a job

Most solo operators price by guessing or copying a competitor. This system prices from **your** numbers, so every job pays you what you need — and you can say no to the ones that don't.

Tool: `08-job-pricing-calculator.xlsx` (three tabs: **Your Numbers**, **Price a Job**, **Rate Card**). Yellow cells are inputs; green cells are results.

---

## Step 1 — Find your target hourly rate (once, then every January)

**Your Numbers** tab:

| Input | Default | Tip |
|-------|---------|-----|
| Annual take-home pay goal | $55,000 | Your salary, not revenue |
| Tax set-aside | 25% | Ask your accountant for your real number |
| Annual business costs | $15,000 | Vehicle, fuel, insurance, supplies, equipment, phone/software, marketing |
| Working weeks per year | 47 | After vacation, weather, sick days |
| Billable hours per week | 28 | Paid, on-site time only — not driving, quoting, or invoicing |

With the defaults: revenue needed ≈ $88,333 ÷ 1,316 billable hours = **$67/hour minimum**, **≈ $77/hour target** with a 15% buffer for slow weeks and callbacks.

> Billable hours are almost always lower than people think. If you work 45 hours a week, 28–32 of them are usually billable.

## Step 2 — Price a job before you say yes

**Price a Job** tab — enter:
- Estimated on-site hours and round-trip drive time
- Materials, disposal/rental/parking fees
- Round-trip miles and your cost per mile
- Card processing %
- The price you're thinking of quoting

It returns:
- **Price needed to hit your target** (never below your minimum job price)
- **Profit per hour of your time** at the price you're considering
- A verdict: *Good price — send it* · *Borderline — tighten scope or raise* · *Too low — raise the price or pass*

Default example: 3 hours on site + 0.5 hours driving, $20 materials, 16 miles → price needed **$315**. At $300 the job pays ~$74/hour of your time → *Borderline*.

## Step 3 — Set your minimum job price

Every job costs a drive, setup, and cleanup before any paid work happens. Your minimum should usually be **1.5–2 × your target hourly rate** (the calculator ships with $125 — change it on the Price a Job tab). Put it on your estimates and stick to it.

## Step 4 — Build your rate card

Use `pricing-sheets.csv` (or the Rate Card tab) as a starting point. For each service you offer:
1. Time yourself on the next 3–5 jobs of that type.
2. Price = typical hours × target rate + typical materials.
3. Round to a clean number. Check it against 2–3 local competitors — if you're far above, look at scope and speed before cutting price.

## Step 5 — Adjust for what makes a job harder

| Situation | Typical adjustment |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Same-day / rush | +20–35% on labor |
| Hard access (stairs, tight gates, tall ceilings, no parking) | +10–25% |
| Heavy condition (buildup, overgrowth, neglect) | +20–50% or hourly |
| Unmanaged pets, clutter, or obstacles | +10–25% |
| Far outside your usual area | Trip fee or higher minimum |

## Step 6 — Say no to bad jobs

Pass (politely) when the Price a Job verdict says "too low" and the customer won't adjust scope:

> "I don't think I'm the right fit at that budget, but I appreciate you asking. If the scope changes, I'd be glad to take another look."

## Review monthly

In your weekly money check-in, the Money Dashboard lists **jobs below your target profit per hour**. If the same service keeps showing up there, raise its price or change how you do it.
