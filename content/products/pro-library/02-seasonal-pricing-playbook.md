# Seasonal Pricing Playbook

Almost every home-service business has a busy season and a slow one. Operators who plan for it raise prices when demand is high, fill slow months on purpose, and save enough in good months that January doesn't hurt.

Tool: `01-seasonal-pricing-planner.xlsx`.

---

## 1. Fill in the planner (20 minutes)

1. **Base price for your typical job** — your most common job at today's price.
2. **Jobs per week when fully booked** — your real capacity, not your best week ever.
3. For each month, pick a **demand level** (Slow / Normal / Busy / Peak) and your usual **% booked**. Use last year's calendar or bank deposits if you have them.
4. Adjust the **price change** for Busy and Peak months (defaults: +5% and +10% for new customers).

The planner shows projected revenue by month, your slowest and busiest months, the gap between them, and **how much to save in each above-average month to cover the below-average ones.**

> These are planning numbers from your own inputs, not predictions.

## 2. Typical seasons by trade

| Trade | Usually busy | Usually slow |
|-------|--------------|--------------|
| Cleaning | Spring, pre-holiday (Nov–Dec), move-out season (May–Aug) | January–February |
| Lawn care | April–June, fall cleanup | Winter (north); mid-summer drought |
| Pressure washing | April–June, September–October | November–February |
| Mobile detailing | Spring (pollen), pre-holiday, post-winter salt | Deep winter, rainy stretches |
| Handyman | Spring, pre-holiday prep, pre-sale home repairs | Late December, mid-summer |

Your area may differ — use your own history.

## 3. Raising prices by season (without losing regulars)

- **New customers first.** Busy-season pricing applies to new bookings. Keep recurring customers on their current rate and give them first pick of dates.
- **Raise before the rush, not during it.** Update your rate card 3–4 weeks before your busy season starts.
- **Round numbers.** $210 reads better than $207.
- **Annual increase for regulars.** Once a year, with 30 days' notice. (See the price increase notices in the Pricing Calculator Pack if you own it, or use the script below.)

**Regular customer notice (text):**
> Hi [Name] — quick heads-up: starting [date], my rate for your [service] goes from $[old] to $[new]. It covers rising fuel and supply costs and lets me keep doing the job right. Thanks for being a regular — I really appreciate you.

## 4. Slow-month plays

Pick one or two, not all of them:
- **Prepay offer:** "Book and prepay 3 visits before [date] and get the 4th at half price."
- **Rebook your best customers:** text everyone you served last busy season.
- **Off-season services:** window cleaning, interior deep cleans, gutter guards, fleet or ceramic packages, indoor handyman lists.
- **Do the business work:** update your rate card, clean up your Google Business Profile, service equipment, and fix your follow-up system.

## 5. Save for the slow months

Each above-average month, move the planner's **"Save this much each busy month"** amount into a separate savings account. That's your slow-season paycheck.
