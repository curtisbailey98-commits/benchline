# Route Planning Guide

For a solo mowing business, **drive time is the biggest hidden cost**. Two operators charging the same prices can earn very different money depending on how tight their routes are. Use this guide with the **Route Planner** sheet.

---

## 1. Measure where you are

Enter every current client in the Route Planner: service day, zone, visit price, on-site minutes, and drive minutes from the previous stop. The day summary shows:

- **Revenue per total hour** (on-site + drive) → compare to your target rate
- **Drive % of day** → aim for **under 20%**

The example rows in the sheet show a common problem: Monday's stops are close together (16% drive), Tuesday's are split between two zones (33% drive), and Tuesday earns much less per hour even though visit prices are similar.

## 2. Build zones

1. Put every client on a map (a free custom map tool works: one pin per client).
2. Draw **3–5 zones**, each small enough to drive across in ~10 minutes.
3. Assign **one zone per day** (big zones may take two days). Example:
   - Mon: North · Tue: East · Wed: Central · Thu: West · Fri: overflow, rain makeup, cleanups · Sat: optional
4. Your home base should be near the middle, or start each day in the farthest zone and work back toward home.

## 3. Set the rules (and stick to them)

| Rule | Why |
|------|-----|
| **New clients must fit an existing zone/day** | One far-away yard can add 30+ minutes of unpaid driving every week |
| **Outside-zone clients pay a premium** (+20–30%) or go on a waitlist | Makes a stray yard worth it, or signals it's time to open a new zone |
| **Minimum stops before opening a new zone:** 8–10 committed clients | A zone with 2 clients is a drive, not a route |
| **Keep Friday as a buffer** | Rain makeups, cleanups, estimates |
| **Order stops as a loop** | No backtracking; right turns where possible |

## 4. Grow density on purpose

- **Neighbor referrals:** text clients when you have openings on their street (script in file 08).
- **Door hangers** only in zones where you already work — 50 doors around your best clients beats 1,000 random doors.
- **Yard signs** at clients' homes (with permission) during the season.
- **Price new far-away requests honestly** — the Route Planner shows what they cost.

## 5. Order of stops within a day

1. Start with the **sunniest, driest** yards (dew burns off there first) and save shaded yards for later in the day.
2. Put properties with gate codes or tight access in the middle of the day (clients more likely to be reachable).
3. End near home or your fuel stop.

## 6. Review monthly

- Any day under your target revenue per hour? → re-order stops, move outliers to a different day, or raise prices on outliers.
- Any client that takes 20%+ longer than priced? → re-price at renewal.
- Waitlist growing in one zone? → raise that zone's prices first.

## 7. Worked example (from the sheet)

| Day | Stops | Revenue | On-site | Drive | $/total hr | Drive % |
|-----|-------|---------|---------|-------|-----------|---------|
| Mon (North only) | 4 | $215 | 2.6 h | 0.5 h | ~$70 | 16% |
| Tue (East + West) | 3 | $155 | 1.8 h | 0.9 h | ~$57 | 33% |

Moving the one West client to a West day (once that zone has enough clients) or pricing it 25% higher closes most of the gap. Both days are also below the default target of ~$87/hour, which means these example visit prices are too low for this operator's goals — exactly what the sheet is meant to reveal.
