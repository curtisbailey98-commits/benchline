# Lawn Care Pricing Guide

Lawn care profit comes from three numbers: **how fast you mow**, **how little you drive**, and **how many visits you sell per client per season**. Price from those, not from the cheapest flyer in the neighborhood.

---

## 1. Your hourly target (season-adjusted)

In **Your Numbers**, set working weeks to your real mowing season (default 36). With $55k take-home, $22k costs, and 35 billable hours/week, the target is about **$87 per mowing hour**. That feels high compared to a laborer's wage because you only earn it ~8–9 months a year and it has to pay for the mower, trailer, fuel, and insurance.

## 2. Price per visit from production rate

Visit time = **turf sq ft ÷ your mowing rate** + **trim/edge/blow time** + **setup time**.

| Mower | Rough production (turf sq ft/hr) |
|-------|-------------------------------|
| 21" push | 8,000–12,000 |
| 30–36" walk-behind | 20,000–30,000 |
| 48–52" stand-on / zero-turn | 45,000–70,000 |

These are rough guides — obstacles, slopes, and wet grass cut them fast. **Time three of your own yards** and put your real rate in the sheet.

With the sheet defaults (20,000 sq ft/hr, 1.0 trim min per 1,000 sq ft, 8 min setup, $87/hr, $40 minimum):

| Lot | Turf | Minutes | Weekly price |
|-----|------|---------|-------------|
| 6,000 sq ft | 3,600 | ~22 | $40 (minimum) |
| 10,000 sq ft | 6,500 | ~34 | $50 |
| 1/2 acre | 16,300 | ~73 | $110 |

**Estimate turf from satellite view.** Use a free map measuring tool on the property's satellite image, subtract house, driveway, beds, and patios.

## 3. Weekly vs. biweekly

Biweekly grass is longer, wetter clippings, more double-cutting. Charge **15–25% more per visit** for biweekly (sheet default +20%), or decline biweekly in peak growth months.

## 4. The minimum visit

Your minimum should cover setup + drive + a few minutes of mowing at your target rate (default $40). Small lots cluster well — they're profitable only when they're next to each other.

## 5. Adjustments

| Condition | Adjustment |
|-----------|------------|
| Steep slopes / ditches | +15–30% or string-trim only |
| Lots of obstacles (trees, beds, play sets) | +10–20% |
| Gated backyard, narrow access | +5 min setup |
| Overgrown first cut (> 6") | +50–100% for that visit |
| Clippings bagged and hauled | +$10–20 per visit |
| Dog waste in yard | Client must pick up before service, or decline |

## 6. Sell the season, not the cut

A **seasonal contract** (see `10-seasonal-maintenance-contract.md` and the **Seasonal Contract** sheet):
- Rolls mowing visits + spring cleanup + mulch/edging + aeration + fall cleanup into one season price
- Bills evenly each month (e.g., 8 payments), so your income doesn't crash in slow weeks
- Offers a prepay discount (default 5%) for cash up front

Default example: 34 mows at $45 + 5 included services ($1,160) = **$2,690/season**, or **$337/month × 8**, or **$2,560 prepaid**.

## 7. Add-on services and how to price them

| Service | Pricing basis | Notes |
|---------|---------------|-------|
| Spring / fall cleanup | Man-hours × rate + disposal | Quote after seeing leaf load |
| Mulch | Labor per cubic yard + material at cost + markup | 1 yard covers ~100 sq ft at 3" deep |
| Bed edging | Per linear foot | Redefine once a year; maintain with trimmer |
| Hedge trimming | Man-hours or per shrub | Haul-away extra |
| Aeration + overseed | Per job (by lot size) + seed | Fall in cool-season grass regions; late spring/early summer for warm-season grass |
| Fertilizer / weed control | Per application | **Applicator license required in many states** |

Starting ranges are in `03-lawn-care-rate-card.csv`.

## 8. Route density is a price lever

A $45 yard next door to an existing client can be worth more than a $70 yard 15 minutes away. Use the **Route Planner** sheet: if a day's revenue per total hour (including drive) is under your target, tighten zones or raise prices in that zone before adding clients.

## 9. Raising prices

Raise once a year before the season starts (Feb), with 30 days' written notice. Fuel and equipment costs are an honest reason. The Price Increase Planner in the Pricing Calculator Pack add-on shows how many clients you could lose and still come out ahead.
